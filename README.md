The Five Core Calculators
1. Day to Day: Count the Days Between Two Dates
The Day to Day calculator takes a start date and an end date and gives you the total number of days, the number of weeks with leftover days, and the working days in between.

Example: from 15 March 2026 to 15 June 2026 is 92 days. Doing that in your head means remembering that March has 31 days, April has 30 and May has 31. The calculator does it in a blink.

People use it to check job tenure, the length of a trip, how long a visa has been active, or how many days are left before an exam.

One honest note: the working-day count skips Saturdays and Sundays only. It does not know about public holidays in your city, so subtract those yourself if they matter for a contract.

2. Days From Today: Find the Exact Date After Any Number of Days
With Days From Today you enter a number and get the target date, the day of the week, and whether it lands on a weekend. You can also go backwards to see what the date was.

3. Weeks From Today: Plan in Blocks of Seven Days
Weeks are easier to plan with because they always keep the same weekday. If you start on a Monday, every block ends on a Monday too. Weeks From Today is handy for agile sprints, coaching programs, college modules and any schedule that repeats weekly.

4. Months From Today: Calendar Months, Handled Properly
A month is not a fixed number of days, and that is where most manual errors begin. Months From Today steps through the real calendar. If you add one month to 31 January 2026, you land on 28 February 2026, because there is no 31 February. Add six months to 31 August 2026 and you get 28 February 2027.

This is the one to use for rent agreements, subscription renewals, EMI schedules and lock-in periods.

5. Years From Today: Long-Range Dates Without Leap-Year Trouble
Years From Today is built for the long view: passport validity, insurance maturity, fixed deposit tenure, retirement targets. It accounts for leap years, so a date four years out does not drift by a day.

Five Mistakes That Cause Wrong Dates
Counting the start day twice. From the 1st to the 10th is 9 days apart, but 10 days if both days count. Decide which rule your document uses.
Treating 30 days as one month. They are different. January 31 plus 30 days is March 2, not February 28.
Forgetting leap years. February has 29 days in 2028 and 2032. Over several years, that extra day moves your answer.
Ignoring weekends. A deadline that falls on a Sunday may really mean Monday or the previous Friday, depending on the contract.
Skipping public holidays. Working-day counts shown by any calculator usually cover weekends only, so check local holidays separately.
Frequently Asked Questions
How do I find the number of days between two dates?
Open the Day to Day calculator, choose a start date and an end date, and read the total days, weeks and working days.

Is 4 weeks the same as 1 month?
No. Four weeks is exactly 28 days, while a calendar month runs 28 to 31 days. Use 4 weeks from today or Months From Today depending on how your deadline is written.

What happens if I add a month to the 31st?
The date moves to the last valid day of the next month. Adding one month to 31 January 2026 gives 28 February 2026.

Do the calculators handle leap years?
Yes. They follow the Gregorian calendar, including 29 February in leap years and the century rules.
